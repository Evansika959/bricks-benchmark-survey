Which public hardware-design benchmarks have every brick?

AI-driven hardware design needs training and evaluation items that are aligned tuples, not isolated files. A complete entry has a natural-language specification, a synthesizable RTL implementation (and, where relevant, the HLS source it came from), a gate-level or golden reference model, a verified testbench, and physical-implementation annotations (power, performance, area).

We surveyed 39 publicly released benchmarks and datasets: logic-synthesis and test suites, LLM-for-RTL benchmarks and corpora, verification benchmarks, HLS suites, PPA and physical-design datasets, and analog/mixed-signal datasets. Each row records which bricks a resource ships and how large it is. Ratings reflect what the released artifacts or the accompanying paper advertise. The proposed BRICKS target is the last row, highlighted.

        How to read the table

        Spec
        Natural-language specification. Shipped means human- or expert-authored. Partial means LLM-generated or incidental documentation.
        HLS
        HLS (C/C++) description.
        RTL
        RTL in Verilog, VHDL or SystemVerilog.
        Gate
        Gate-level netlist.
        TB
        Testbench. Partial means a self-checking harness, assertions or a formal checker rather than a full reference testbench.
        PPA
        Power, performance and area annotations. Shipped means post-place-and-route or post-layout. Partial means post-synthesis or post-HLS estimates only.
        Size
        + up to roughly 200 items, or mostly small blocks. ++ hundreds to tens of thousands of items, or designs up to roughly 100K gates. +++ more than 100K items, or million-gate designs. Grouped rows share identical ratings on all six bricks, so the size column shows the range.
